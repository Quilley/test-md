Follow the work until it is finished.

A café path used to show how a picture can carry the explanation: what builds up, where the limit sits, and who hands work to whom.

What limits completed output?

When work passes through several steps, the busiest step is not always the one that limits completion. A drink can be made and still wait for a lid, a name or a handoff.

Each figure below answers one question about the same path. All rates are constructed for this example and are not observations.

Unfinished work is a level that rises.

Orders arrive faster than handoff completes them, so the difference collects. Pipe thickness shows each rate. The hatched level shows what has collected.

The smallest capacity sets the limit.

Preparation can finish 8 orders a minute and handoff 4. Completed flow cannot exceed the smaller number, whatever the other steps do.

Unfinished work grows by the gap.

With handoff at 4 a minute, nothing builds up at 4 arrivals. At 6, two extra orders join each minute. At 8, four do.

Follow one order across the people who touch it.

Columns are the people or steps involved and rows run downward in time. The number on each step gives the order, so the sequence survives on a narrow screen.

Each part of the path has a role.

Numbers on the picture point to the key beside it. Select a number to jump to its note.

Compare changes at a glance.

Each filled share is a fraction of a full circle. The word beside it says the same thing for anyone who cannot see the fill.

Notes

1. Every rate and rating is constructed for this example. No empirical claim about a real café is supplied.

2. For a serial path with constant rates and no lost orders, completed flow is the smaller of the step capacities and arrivals. Unfinished work grows by arrivals minus completed flow: 6 − 4 = 2 orders a minute.
