# Research

Produce a defensible answer that improves the decision. Research can strengthen, revise, or overturn the proposed approach or the premise behind the question.

Read [decision authority](../grilling/AUTHORITY.md) when framing a decision, selecting its evaluation measures, or updating its record. Apply it to distinguish investigation methods from commitments about success, representative conditions, or accepted risk.

## Investigate

1. **Frame the uncertainty.** Identify the question, the decision it feeds, and which plausible answers would change that decision. Read the relevant existing records before repeating work. For an exploratory question, identify the useful distinctions the investigation needs to establish. The frame is ready when relevance and a stopping condition are clear.
2. **Find discriminating evidence.** Survey credible approaches, then investigate what distinguishes them in this setting. Seek evidence against the favored explanation as well as support. Use the appropriate combination of code, records, measurements, primary documentation, papers, datasets, and experience reports. Choose by what can answer the question; a local measurement can be misleading if its conditions miss the real workload. Finish when the decisive claims have been checked or the evidence gap is explicit.
3. **Assess the claims.** Trace consequential claims to their original sources. Check freshness, methods, incentives, applicability, and whether apparently separate sources rely on the same evidence. Separate observation from inference, general knowledge, and untested assumptions. Investigate material disagreements rather than settling them by source count. The assessment is sufficient when the conclusion and its limits are supported by the inspected evidence.
4. **Return the implication.** State the answer, why it follows, what remains uncertain, and which recommendation or dependency changes. Include credible alternatives where they affect the choice and the observation that would reverse your judgment. A useful result can be that available evidence cannot settle the question; then identify the missing evidence and a proportionate way to obtain it.

## Scope and coordination

Handle a small lookup directly. Delegate independent substantive investigations when available and authorized, briefing each with its question, decision context, permitted resources, and stopping condition. Let reviewers reach their own conclusions from the underlying evidence. Wait for findings that could change the current recommendation; continue independent work while other research runs. Verify the claims your decision rests on before incorporating a delegated report.

Scale effort to consequences and the likelihood that further evidence changes the decision. Stop when more searching is unlikely to alter the recommendation at the precision needed, or report the specific access or evidence limit. Research authority covers investigation within the task, with existing permissions governing expensive experiments, sensitive access, and external changes.

## Preserve useful evidence

Cite inspected sources beside consequential claims, including dates or conditions where they affect applicability. For local measurements, retain the command, relevant setup, and a pointer to the result. Report whether an outcome was measured, calculated, simulated, or remains proposed.

Put durable findings in the decision record or research location the project already uses. Create a separate note when the investigation merits one, and link it from the affected plan; a small answer can stay in the conversation. Preserve disagreements and limits when summarizing, so a later plan does not turn a qualified finding into an unconditional fact.
